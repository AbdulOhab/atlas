# Leveling Up

Refactoring UI ends not with more rules but with two ways to keep generating your own — the difference between having read the book and having absorbed it.

> **Note:** This module is a study guide written for Atlas CE, not a copy. Refactoring UI is a commercial book by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger — buy it at [refactoringui.com](https://www.refactoringui.com). Each section below distills one idea from the book's *Leveling Up* chapter.

## Look for Decisions You Wouldn't Have Made

Whenever you hit a design you admire, ask one question: **did the designer do anything here I never would have thought to do?**

Not "is this pretty?" — that's taste confirmation. The question hunts for *unintuitive moves*, because those are the ones carrying information you don't have yet:

- A datepicker that inverts the background color instead of outlining the selected day.
- A button placed *inside* a text input rather than beside it.
- A headline set in two different colors instead of one.

Each answer is a trick you can file and apply elsewhere. Screens you already like are full of these; the question is what makes them visible.

**The norm:** study designs you admire for the decisions that wouldn't have occurred to you — that gap is the lesson.

## Rebuild Your Favorite Interfaces

The single best way to notice the invisible details of a polished design is to **recreate it from scratch, without opening the developer tools**. Pixel-match it by eye.

When your version sits next to the original and something's off, you don't get the answer handed to you — you have to find it, and that search is where the learning happens. This is how you discover, on your own, the very rules this track has been distilling: tighter line-height on headings, letter-spacing on uppercase text, two-part shadows, the exact shade one step darker.

Iterating "why does theirs look right and mine look wrong" dozens of times builds an eye that no checklist can.

**The norm:** clone interfaces by eye; the differences you're forced to explain become permanent intuition.
